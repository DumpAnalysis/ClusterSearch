Definitions

Terms as used on this page.

Spectrum
A list of (m/z, intensity) pairs sorted by m/z. Profile data samples a continuous signal (many points per peak); centroid data has one point per peak. Auto mode classifies a spectrum as profile when it has at least 200 points and the median spacing of consecutive points is below 0.1.
Peak (profile data)
A local maximum of the sampled intensity that is at or above the threshold (given as a percentage of the base peak, the most intense sample) and whose prominence — apex intensity minus the higher of the two minima reached on either side before the signal exceeds the apex again — is at least the given percentage of the apex. Its m/z is the vertex of the parabola fitted through the natural logarithm of the apex sample and its two neighbours (exact for a Gaussian peak); its intensity is the apex sample. FWHM is the full width at half the apex intensity (linear interpolation between samples) and the resolving power is R = (m/z)/FWHM. For centroid data every point at or above the threshold is a peak.
Isotope envelope
A maximal run of consecutive detected peaks in which neighbours are at most 2.15/|z| apart in m/z. Its apex is the most intense member. Its centroid is the intensity‑weighted mean m/z: for profile data computed from the raw samples over the envelope extended by 4.4/|z| on each side (two isotopologue spacings, to include tail isotopologues below the threshold) after subtracting a baseline equal to the 20th percentile of the samples in that window; for centroid data from the member peaks. Envelopes truncated by the threshold, or overlapped by another species, have biased centroids; lowering the threshold or using apex nodes helps.
Composition, cluster
A composition is a multiset of atoms, written as a formula such as Ag5O; a cluster ion is a charged composition. The composition search enumerates all compositions of the chosen elements within the given count ranges, keeps those whose isotope pattern has a peak within tolerance of the target m/z (by default the most abundant isotopologue; optionally any isotopologue of at least 5 % relative abundance), and ranks them by pattern fit. The enumeration is pruned with bounds from the lightest and heaviest isotope of each element and stops after 200 000 evaluated compositions.
Isotope pattern (isotopologue distribution)
The probability distribution of the mass of a composition over isotopologues, computed from natural isotopic abundances by convolution (element powers by repeated squaring). Peaks closer than the merge width are merged into one at their abundance‑weighted mass; peaks below 10−7 of the maximum in the calculator (10−4 during searches, where the merge width is 0.005 u) are dropped. Relative abundance is scaled to the most abundant peak = 100 %; probability is the fraction of all isotopologues. Elements without a standard atomic weight are treated as consisting of the single listed isotope.
Masses
Monoisotopic mass: sum over atoms of the mass of the most abundant isotope of each element (the same convention as the periodic table page). Average mass: sum of standard atomic weights (IUPAC 2021; the conventional value where an interval is given). Nominal mass: sum of the mass numbers of the most abundant isotopes. Most abundant m/z: m/z of the most probable isotopologue. The centroid of an isotope pattern equals its average mass.
m/z and charge
For a composition of mass M and signed charge z: m/z = (M − z·me)/|z| with the electron mass me = 0.000548580 u (CODATA 2018); cations (z > 0) have lost electrons, anions gained them. z = 0 denotes a neutral mass (used for a series core).
Tolerance
Absolute, in m/z units (labelled Da), or relative, in parts per million of the target m/z. Δ is reported as observed − calculated, in mDa and ppm.
Pattern fit
Cosine similarity between the vector of theoretical relative abundances (isotopologues ≥ 1 %) and the vector of observed intensities of the nearest detected peak within tolerance of each isotopologue (0 where none is found). 1.000 means proportional intensities; it is insensitive to overall scale. Coverage is the fraction of theoretical abundance for which a peak was found.
Cluster series
A set of nodes (envelope centroids, envelope apexes or peaks) at m/zk = m/z0 + k·Δ/|z| for k = 0, 1, 2, …, where Δ is the unit mass: the average mass of the unit formula (appropriate for envelope centroids), its monoisotopic mass (appropriate for individual peaks of monoisotopic or light elements), or a custom value. Chains are grown from the lightest unused node by stepping +Δ/|z| and accepting the nearest node within tolerance; with “missing allowed”, up to that many consecutive steps may be empty. A least‑squares fit of m/z against k gives the measured spacing and its residual standard deviation. The first member's neutral mass M0 is decomposed as n·Δ + core: when M0 is within tolerance of a multiple of Δ the series is a pure cluster series Un; otherwise the core (M0 mod Δ) is the mass that does not belong to the repeating unit and can be searched as a neutral composition.
Spacings
All pairwise m/z differences among the 60 most intense nodes; the densest windows of width 2 × tolerance containing at least three differences are listed (greedily by count, non‑overlapping) with elements and common units whose average or monoisotopic mass matches the spacing (× |z|) within tolerance, or a multiple k× of it (tolerance/k).

Method. Peaks: local maxima above a threshold with a minimum prominence; m/z from the log‑parabola vertex through the apex and its neighbours. Isotope patterns: convolution of isotopic abundance distributions with abundance‑weighted merging of peaks closer than the merge width. Composition search: bounded enumeration of element counts, candidates kept when an isotopologue falls within tolerance of the target, ranked by cosine similarity between theoretical and observed intensities. Series: chains of nodes at constant m/z spacing, spacing refined by least squares. See Definitions for the exact terms. m/z = (M − z·me)/|z|, me = 5.485 799 09 × 10−4 u (CODATA 2018). Settings are kept in this browser's local storage.

Data sources. Isotopic masses: AME2020 (Wang et al. 2021). Isotopic abundances: CIAAW “Isotopic compositions of the elements 2021”; where CIAAW gives a range, the representative value is used. Standard atomic weights: IUPAC 2021 (Prohaska et al. 2022), shown to five significant figures in the table and in full in the details; for elements whose standard atomic weight is an interval, the conventional value is used; a bracketed value is the mass number of the longest-lived isotope of an element with no stable isotopes. All three via the periodictable Python package v2.1.0, except uranium abundances, which that package omits and which are taken directly from CIAAW representative values.

Element properties (electron configuration, electronegativity, phase, melting/boiling points, density, ionization energy, electron affinity, molar heat, descriptions): Bowserinator/Periodic-Table-JSON, a Wikipedia-derived dataset; treat those values as approximate. Monoisotopic mass here means the mass of the most abundant naturally occurring isotope.
